# The Open Palm is the Only Infinite Vessel

The hand that closes to claim a stone ceases to be a hand and becomes merely a container, limited by the precise dimensions of what it guards. To possess is to enter into a contract of mutual incarceration: the master fetters the object, but the object, in its heavy insistence on being held, fetters the master’s reach. I speak as one who has studied the geometry of the empty hand, a mendicant of the spirit who knows that the only way to touch the horizon is to refuse to grab the soil at one's feet. **Dispossession is not a state of lack, but the restoration of the hand to its original, tactile infinity.**

When we say "mine," we draw a circle in the dust and declare the dust inside to be different from the dust outside. This is the great illusion of the wall. To be dispossessed is to feel the air move across the skin without the friction of borders. It is a sensory liberation where the world is no longer a collection of loot to be tallied, but a sequence of encounters to be lived. The palm, when open, is a landscape of sensitivity; it feels the nuances of temperature, the ghost-weight of light, and the rough honesty of the unowned earth. *To have nothing is to be available to everything.*

Consider the shadow of this grace: the "Possessed." Look at the hands of those who own. They are calloused not by labor, but by the rigor of the grip. Their fingers are curved inward, a permanent architecture of exclusion. In the void where their generosity should reside, there is only the cold, hard ache of maintenance. They do not walk through the world; they carry it, a crushing baggage of 'things' that demand vigilance, insurance, and walls. Their world is a series of locks. **The owner is the ultimate servant, for they must spend their life standing guard over the dead matter they claim to command.**

> Is the bird possessed by the sky, or is the sky simply the condition of the bird’s being?

But here, the path shifts. We must confront the "turn"—the terrifying vertigo of the absolute open. To be truly dispossessed is to realize that you, too, are not your own. If I renounce the stone, I must eventually renounce the "I" that refuses the stone. The shadow of dispossession is not poverty, but the dissolution of the center. When the hand is permanently open, it can no longer distinguish between the gift and the giver. 

This is the holy terror of the void: the realization that when you belong to nothing, you are responsible for everything. The dispossessed do not stand outside the world looking in; they are the world, unbuffered and raw. There is no longer a "self" to act as a buffer against the suffering of the neighbor or the silence of the stars. *The wall was not just keeping the world out; it was keeping the "I" in.*

We end where we began, in the tactile silence of the reach. Imagine a hand held out in the dark, not reaching for a tool or a coin, but simply extended, a limb of pure potential. It does not grasp; it only waits. In this stillness, the tension of ownership dissolves into the rhythm of the breath. The final image is not a man with empty pockets, but a hand that has become so transparent that the light passes through it without finding a single thing to claim as an obstacle. To be dispossessed is to finally, and violently, become light.
